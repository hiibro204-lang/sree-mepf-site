Fire safety: from the tank to the head that opens.

The only division where a failed functional test stops a building from opening. Every number on this path is inspected, so every number is designed and documented before we install it.

How a fire system responds

Responsesuppression, dampers, lift homing
The pump set is three pressure bands, not one pump. The jockey holds pressure and starts on a small drop; the main pump starts on a further drop and stops only by hand; the standby takes over on failure or low pressure. That logic is what an inspector tests first.

Inspection readiness

What the committee actually does

  • Opens the farthest landing valve and checks the pump starts
  • Confirms the flow switch shows at the panel
  • Runs cause and effect end to end - alarm, dampers, AHU shutdown, lift homing
  • Measures gate width and head clearance for a fire tender
  • Walks the means of escape, emergency lighting and exit signage
  • Asks for as-builts that match what is installed
Sequence

Order that keeps the test honest

  • Mains and risers, pump room and tanks
  • Branch lines and heads after ceiling coordination
  • Alarm cabling, panel and cause-and-effect programming
  • Hydraulic test, then flow test at the farthest outlet
  • Witnessed functional test with the consultant and the client
  • Handover pack: test records, pump curve, as-builts, O and M
Standards: NBC 2016 Part 4, IS 15105:2021 (sprinklers), IS 15301 (fire pumps), IS 2189 practice for detection, and NFPA 13 / 14 / 72 where the specification follows NFPA.

Building height, occupancy and plot area?
